📅 Origin

Āryabhaṭīya (499 CE), Gaṇitapāda verses 32–33. Āryabhaṭa (b. 476 CE) formulated this as a systematic algorithm—predating European equivalents by over a millennium.

🔤 Etymology

Kuṭṭaka = "Pulverizer" or "Breaker." The name describes the repeated division that gradually reduces numbers, like grinding spices to reveal their hidden essence.

🌌 Purpose

Originally developed for astronomical calculations: computing planetary conjunctions, determining calendar cycles (Mahāyuga), and finding elapsed days in cosmic time-cycles.

👨‍🏫 Later Scholars

Bhāskara I wrote the first detailed commentary. Brahmagupta generalized it. Bhāskara II further refined the method.

🌍 Global Impact

Predates Euler's algorithm (1763) and connects directly to the modern Chinese Remainder Theorem, modular arithmetic, and RSA cryptography. A cornerstone of number theory.

🌿 The Vallī

The Vallī (creeper) table organizes quotients into a climbing vine-like structure, propagating solutions backwards — a beautiful metaphor for emergence of order from apparent chaos.
